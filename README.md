## Overview

This case study presents my product-management involvement in an AI-powered B2B SaaS product focused on energy analytics and operational insights.

The product was designed to help organizations better understand complex energy-related information, explore performance across different levels of their building infrastructure, and use structured insights to support data-driven operational decisions.

My involvement covered multiple stages of the product lifecycle, including:

- Product discovery
- Problem and user-need analysis
- AI opportunity exploration
- Product requirements
- User stories and acceptance criteria
- UX and user workflows
- Dashboard and analytics concepts
- Solution definition
- Product delivery
- Product metrics and KPIs
- Product learning and decision-making

---

## Business Context

The product initiative focused on improving how organizations understand and work with complex energy-related information across their building infrastructure.

From a product perspective, I worked on shaping platform capabilities that could help users:

- Monitor and understand energy performance through structured dashboards and metrics
- Navigate energy information across different levels of a building hierarchy
- Analyze historical energy consumption and identify meaningful trends
- Compare performance across different areas of a building or portfolio
- Access more detailed information for deeper analysis and investigation
- Generate standardized reports for different business and operational needs
- Organize and present energy-related information to support faster decision-making
- Explore opportunities where AI could assist with analysis and insight generation

The product work involved translating these needs into product requirements, user workflows, dashboard concepts, reporting capabilities, KPIs, and prioritized product initiatives.

---

## The Product Challenge

The key product challenge was to transform complex energy-related information into a product experience that could be easily understood and used by different types of users.

From my product perspective, the major challenges included:

- **Information Complexity** — Structuring large amounts of energy-related information into understandable product experiences.
- **Multiple Levels of Analysis** — Supporting analysis from high-level building views to more granular areas of the infrastructure.
- **Actionable Insights** — Moving beyond simply displaying data toward helping users identify meaningful patterns and areas requiring attention.
- **Reporting Requirements** — Defining reporting experiences that could present relevant information in a standardized and useful format.
- **User Experience** — Designing intuitive navigation and workflows for users with different information needs.
- **AI Opportunity** — Identifying areas where AI could reduce analysis effort and make complex information easier to interpret.
- **Product Prioritization** — Balancing user needs, business priorities, product roadmap, and delivery feasibility.

## Metrics & KPIs

A key part of the product work was identifying product-level KPIs to understand adoption, engagement, and the overall effectiveness of the product experience.

The product success framework considered metrics such as:

- **User Acquisition Rate** — Measuring growth in new users or customer accounts
- **Activation Rate** — Measuring how effectively new users reach meaningful product adoption
- **User Retention Rate** — Understanding whether users continue to engage with the product over time
- **Product Adoption Rate** — Measuring adoption of core product capabilities
- **Feature Adoption Rate** — Understanding usage of newly introduced or priority features
- **User Engagement** — Measuring the frequency and depth of product interaction
- **Conversion Rate** — Tracking movement from initial interest or usage toward desired product actions
- **Customer/User Growth** — Monitoring overall growth of the product user base
- **Time to Value** — Understanding how quickly users reach meaningful value from the product
- **Task/Workflow Completion Rate** — Measuring whether users successfully complete key product workflows

I focused on connecting product capabilities with measurable user and business outcomes rather than evaluating success only through the underlying domain data.

## Product Thinking & Decision-Making

Product decisions were approached by balancing user needs, business value, delivery feasibility, and product priorities.

### Key Areas of Focus

- Prioritizing capabilities based on user value and business impact
- Breaking larger requirements into manageable product increments
- Balancing immediate business requests with longer-term roadmap priorities
- Evaluating feature scope against feasibility and delivery effort
- Using feedback and product insights to refine requirements
- Defining clear outcomes and acceptance criteria
- Making trade-offs while maintaining stakeholder and delivery-team alignment

The goal was to ensure that product decisions were driven by broader product value rather than individual feature requests alone.

---

## Key Product Learnings

Working on a complex B2B product reinforced several important product-management principles:

- **Complex problems need simple experiences** — Product value often depends on how effectively complex information is presented and understood.
- **AI should solve a real problem** — AI is most valuable when it addresses a genuine user need and improves an existing workflow or experience.
- **Context matters in product decisions** — The value of a capability depends on the user's role, objective, and level of interaction.
- **Good requirements create delivery clarity** — Clear requirements, acceptance criteria, and collaboration help reduce ambiguity throughout delivery.
- **Product decisions require trade-offs** — Strong outcomes come from balancing user value, business priorities, feasibility, and long-term product direction.
