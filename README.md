## Overview

This case study presents my product-management involvement in shaping an AI-powered B2B SaaS product focused on energy analytics and operational insights.

The product was designed to help organizations better understand complex energy-related information, explore performance across different levels of their building infrastructure, identify meaningful patterns, and support data-driven operational decision-making.

My involvement covered multiple stages of the product lifecycle, including:

- Product discovery
- Problem and opportunity definition
- AI opportunity exploration
- Product requirements
- User stories and acceptance criteria
- UX and user workflows
- Dashboard and analytics thinking
- Solution definition
- Product delivery
- Product metrics and KPIs
- Product learning and reflection

---

## Business Context

The product initiative focused on improving how organizations understand and work with complex energy-related information across their building infrastructure.

From a product perspective, the platform needed to help users:

- Monitor and understand energy performance
- Navigate information across different levels of a building hierarchy
- Analyze energy consumption and identify meaningful trends
- Compare performance across different areas
- Drill down into more detailed information when required
- Generate standardized reports
- Organize complex information into usable product experiences
- Reduce manual analysis effort
- Explore practical applications of AI for analysis and insight generation

The product work involved translating these needs into structured requirements, user workflows, dashboard concepts, reporting capabilities, KPIs, and prioritized product initiatives.

---

## The Product Challenge

The central product challenge was:

> **How can complex energy-related information be transformed into a simple, intuitive, and actionable B2B product experience?**

The challenge involved several dimensions:

### 1. Information Complexity

Large amounts of energy-related information needed to be organized into experiences that different users could understand and use effectively.

### 2. Multiple Levels of Analysis

Users needed to move between high-level views and more granular levels of information depending on their objective.

### 3. Actionable Insights

The product needed to go beyond simply displaying data and help users identify meaningful patterns, trends, and areas requiring attention.

### 4. Reporting

Users required structured reporting experiences that could present relevant information in a consistent and useful format.

### 5. User Experience

The platform needed intuitive navigation and workflows that could support different user objectives without unnecessary complexity.

### 6. AI Opportunity

AI needed to be evaluated based on genuine product problems and potential user value rather than being introduced simply as a technology feature.

### 7. Product Prioritization

Product decisions required balancing user needs, business priorities, feasibility, dependencies, delivery effort, and longer-term product direction.

# Product Approach

I approached the product work as an iterative process rather than treating product development as a linear requirements exercise.

The overall approach was:

**Discovery → Opportunity → Requirements → UX → Solution → Delivery → Measurement → Learning**

Each stage was connected to the next so that product decisions remained traceable back to user problems and expected outcomes.

# Product Thinking & Decision-Making

Product decisions were approached by balancing:

- User value
- Business impact
- Product priorities
- Delivery feasibility
- Expected effort
- Dependencies
- Stakeholder needs
- Long-term product direction

### Decision-Making Principles

#### Prioritize Outcomes Over Individual Requests

Feature requests were evaluated based on the broader problem and expected value rather than being treated as isolated requirements.

#### Break Complexity Into Manageable Increments

Large requirements were broken into smaller product increments that could be delivered and validated iteratively.

#### Balance Short-Term and Long-Term Needs

Immediate business requests were considered alongside longer-term product and roadmap priorities.

#### Make Explicit Trade-offs

Scope and feature decisions considered value, feasibility, effort, dependencies, and product impact.

#### Keep Teams Aligned

Product, design, engineering, and business stakeholders were brought into discussions around requirements, scope, feasibility, and expected outcomes.

# Product Learning

Working on a complex B2B product reinforced several important product-management principles.

### Complex Problems Need Simple Experiences

The value of a complex product often depends on how effectively complexity is structured and presented to the user.

### AI Should Solve a Real Problem

AI creates stronger product value when it addresses a genuine user need or improves an existing workflow.

### Context Matters

The usefulness of a capability depends on the user's role, objective, workflow, and required level of interaction.

### Good Requirements Create Delivery Clarity

Clear requirements, acceptance criteria, and cross-functional collaboration reduce ambiguity during product delivery.

### Product Decisions Require Trade-offs

Strong product outcomes require balancing user value, business priorities, feasibility, delivery effort, and long-term product direction.

---

# My Product Management Takeaways

This case study strengthened my approach to product management in several areas:

- Start with the problem rather than the feature
- Understand the user context before defining the solution
- Convert ambiguous problems into structured product opportunities
- Use requirements to create clarity without over-prescribing implementation
- Treat UX as part of product thinking, not only visual design
- Evaluate AI through user value and business outcomes
- Use metrics to understand product effectiveness
- Make prioritization decisions using value, impact, feasibility, and effort
- Maintain alignment between discovery, requirements, design, engineering, and delivery
- Learn continuously from feedback and product outcomes
