# UX & User Workflows

## Purpose

The UX work focused on translating product requirements into clear and intuitive user workflows.

The objective was to make complex information easier to navigate, understand, and use while keeping the experience aligned with the needs of different users.

---

## UX Approach

My approach focused on:

- Understanding how users need to access and consume information
- Structuring information based on user priorities
- Defining clear navigation paths
- Reducing unnecessary steps in key workflows
- Translating requirements into user flows and dashboard concepts
- Collaborating with design teams during solution definition
- Using feedback to refine the experience

The focus was on creating an experience that supported the user's objective rather than simply displaying more information.

---

## Information Architecture

The product required information to be organized across different levels of detail.

From a product perspective, I worked on defining:

- Logical information hierarchy
- Navigation between different levels
- Relevant information at each level
- Drill-down paths for deeper analysis
- Relationships between dashboards, analytical views, and reports
- Consistent navigation patterns

This helped users move from a broader overview toward more detailed information when required.

---

## Key User Workflows

The major workflows considered included:

### 1. Explore Information

Users should be able to start from a high-level view and navigate toward more detailed information based on their objective.

**Goal:** Reduce the effort required to find relevant information.

### 2. Analyze Information

Users should be able to review relevant analytical views and historical information to understand patterns and changes.

**Goal:** Support meaningful analysis rather than simple data consumption.

### 3. Compare Information

Users should be able to compare relevant areas or periods when comparison is required.

**Goal:** Help users identify differences, trends, and areas requiring attention.

### 4. Generate Reports

Users should be able to access structured reporting workflows based on their information needs.

**Goal:** Make reporting more consistent and easier to use.

## Dashboard & Analytical Experience

I worked with design and engineering teams to shape dashboard concepts around:

- Relevant information hierarchy
- Key metrics and indicators
- Appropriate visualization choices
- Analytical context
- Drill-down experiences
- Comparison views
- Time-based analysis
- Clear user actions

The objective was to ensure that dashboards supported specific user decisions rather than becoming information-heavy screens.

## Iterative Refinement

UX decisions were refined through discussions, feedback, and validation.

Areas considered during refinement included:

- Navigation complexity
- Information discoverability
- Number of steps in a workflow
- Clarity of information
- Consistency across screens

The objective was to continuously simplify the experience while preserving the information users needed.

## Product Perspective

A key principle from the UX work was:

**Good UX is not about showing more information; it is about helping users reach the right information with less effort.**

My focus was therefore on connecting user needs, product requirements, information architecture, and workflows into a coherent product experience.

## UX Decision Principles

UX decisions were guided by the underlying user workflow rather than by individual screens or features.

The key principles were:

### 1. Start With the User's Objective

Each workflow was considered from the perspective of what the user was trying to accomplish rather than what functionality the platform could expose.

### 2. Progressive Disclosure

Users should be able to start with a high-level understanding and move into deeper information when required.

This helps manage complexity without removing access to detailed information.

### 3. Contextual Navigation

Navigation and drill-down experiences should preserve enough context for users to understand where they are and why they are viewing a particular level of information.

### 4. Minimize Unnecessary Steps

Important workflows should avoid unnecessary navigation and interactions wherever possible.

The objective was to reduce friction between identifying a question and reaching the information needed to investigate it.

### 5. Design Around Workflows

Analytics, comparison, reporting, and other capabilities should support connected user workflows rather than operate as isolated features.

### 6. Validate Through Feedback

UX decisions should be refined through stakeholder feedback, user feedback, testing, and observation of workflow friction.
