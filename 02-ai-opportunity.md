# AI Opportunity Exploration

## Objective

As part of the product work, I explored how AI could be applied to improve the way users analyze and interpret complex energy-related information.

The objective was not to introduce AI simply as a technology feature, but to identify practical opportunities where AI could reduce user effort, improve understanding, and support better product experiences.

---

## Why AI Was Considered

The product involved complex information and analytical workflows that could require users to review multiple data points and identify meaningful patterns.

This created opportunities to explore whether AI could help users:

- Reduce repetitive analysis
- Interpret complex information more efficiently
- Surface relevant patterns and insights
- Improve the overall information experience
- Support users in moving from data toward actionable understanding

---

## AI Opportunity Areas

The exploration focused on several potential opportunity areas:

| Opportunity | Potential User Value |
|---|---|
| Automated Analysis | Reduce repetitive manual analysis |
| Insight Generation | Help users identify meaningful patterns |
| Information Interpretation | Make complex information easier to understand |
| Contextual Assistance | Provide relevant information based on user needs |
| Workflow Support | Reduce effort within analytical workflows |
| Decision Support | Help users interpret information more effectively |

---

## Product Approach to AI

AI opportunities were evaluated from a product perspective rather than a technology-first perspective.

The evaluation considered:

1. What user problem does the capability solve?
2. How frequently does the problem occur?
3. How much manual effort could be reduced?
4. What value would the capability provide to users?
5. What level of accuracy would be required?
6. How should users interact with the AI capability?
7. How should AI-generated outputs be presented?
8. How could the output be evaluated and improved?

This helped keep the AI exploration focused on practical product value.

---

## User-Centric AI Thinking

A key principle was that AI should improve an existing user workflow rather than add unnecessary complexity.

Potential AI capabilities were therefore considered in terms of:

- User intent
- Context of use
- Expected output
- Accuracy requirements
- User trust
- Explainability
- Ease of interaction
- Business value

The goal was to make AI useful within the product experience rather than treating it as a standalone feature.

---

## AI Product Considerations

Several product considerations were identified while exploring AI opportunities:

### Accuracy

AI-generated insights should be sufficiently reliable for the intended use case.

### Trust

Users should understand when information is AI-generated and be able to assess its relevance.

### Context

AI outputs should be based on relevant product and user context rather than generic responses.

### Usability

AI interactions should remain simple and fit naturally into existing workflows.

### Human Oversight

AI should support users rather than unnecessarily replace human judgment, particularly for important operational decisions.

### Evaluation

AI capabilities should have defined criteria for measuring usefulness, accuracy, relevance, and user adoption.

---

## Opportunity Evaluation Framework

Potential AI capabilities were considered using a combination of:

| Evaluation Area | Key Question |
|---|---|
| User Value | Does this solve a meaningful user problem? |
| Frequency | How often does the problem occur? |
| Effort Reduction | Can AI meaningfully reduce manual work? |
| Feasibility | Can the capability be delivered reliably? |
| Accuracy | What level of accuracy is required? |
| Risk | What could happen if the output is incorrect? |
| Adoption | Would users actually use the capability? |
| Business Value | Does the opportunity support a meaningful product outcome? |

---

## Product Decision-Making

AI opportunities were not treated equally.

The product thinking involved balancing:

- User value
- Business impact
- Technical feasibility
- Data availability
- Accuracy requirements
- Implementation effort
- Product priorities
- Potential risks

This helped distinguish between AI capabilities that could provide meaningful product value and ideas that were interesting from a technology perspective but less valuable to users.

---

## Key Product Learnings

The AI exploration reinforced several principles:

- **AI should solve a real problem** rather than exist simply as a product feature.
- **User context matters** when determining what AI should provide.
- **Accuracy and trust are product requirements**, not only technical concerns.
- **AI outputs need clear evaluation criteria** to determine whether they are actually useful.
- **Human judgment remains important** for workflows involving significant business decisions.
- **The best AI opportunities reduce friction** within an existing workflow.

---

## AI Opportunity Evaluation

AI opportunities were evaluated from a product perspective rather than being prioritized solely because they were technically possible.

The evaluation considered five dimensions:

| Dimension | Key Question |
|---|---|
| User Value | Does the opportunity solve a meaningful user problem? |
| Business Value | Can it contribute to an important product or business outcome? |
| Feasibility | Can the capability be implemented reliably within the available constraints? |
| Workflow Fit | Does AI naturally fit into an existing user workflow? |
| Trust & Risk | Can users understand, validate, and appropriately rely on the output? |

This helped distinguish opportunities where AI could create meaningful value from opportunities where traditional product functionality would be simpler or more appropriate.

## Outcome

The AI exploration helped identify practical areas where AI could potentially improve the product experience while keeping the focus on user value, usability, feasibility, and responsible product decision-making.

The exercise also strengthened the product direction around evaluating AI as a capability within a broader B2B SaaS workflow rather than treating AI as an isolated technology feature.
