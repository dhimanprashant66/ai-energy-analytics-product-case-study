# Solution Design

## Solution Overview

The solution was designed as a B2B SaaS product for helping users understand, analyze, compare, and act on complex energy-related information through a structured digital experience.

The product approach focused on reducing information complexity while providing users with the appropriate level of detail for different analytical and operational needs.

The solution connected the outcomes from product discovery, AI opportunity exploration, requirements definition, and user workflows into a coherent product experience.

---

## Product Solution Principles

The solution was guided by the following principles:

- Make complex information easier to understand
- Present information in a structured and contextual way
- Support movement from high-level information to deeper analysis
- Reduce unnecessary navigation and manual effort
- Keep analytical workflows focused on user objectives
- Provide actionable information rather than information overload
- Introduce AI where it can create meaningful user value
- Maintain flexibility for incremental product development

---

## Core Product Capabilities

The solution was organized around several core capability areas:

| Capability | Product Purpose |
|---|---|
| Information Overview | Provide users with a clear understanding of the current situation |
| Analytics | Enable exploration and interpretation of relevant information |
| Navigation | Support movement across different levels of detail |
| Comparison | Help users identify meaningful differences and patterns |
| Reporting | Provide structured reporting workflows |
| AI Assistance | Reduce repetitive analysis and surface relevant insights |
| Product Analytics | Measure adoption and user engagement |

---

## Information Architecture

The product experience was structured to allow users to move from broader information toward more detailed analysis.

A simplified information flow was:

**Overview → Context → Detailed Analysis → Comparison → Insight → Report**

This structure was intended to help users understand the broader situation before moving into deeper analytical workflows.

The information architecture also considered that different users may require different levels of detail depending on their objectives.

---

## Dashboard Experience

The dashboard was considered the primary entry point into the product experience.

The dashboard was designed to help users:

- Understand key information quickly
- Identify important trends and patterns
- Navigate to deeper analysis
- Access relevant analytical views
- Move into comparison workflows
- Access reporting capabilities
- Identify areas requiring further investigation

The objective was to provide sufficient context at the entry point without overwhelming users with excessive information.

---

## Analytics Experience

The analytics experience was designed around exploration and interpretation rather than simply displaying raw information.

Key considerations included:

- Presenting relevant information in a structured format
- Supporting different levels of analytical detail
- Helping users identify trends and patterns
- Allowing users to investigate areas of interest
- Connecting analytical views with the broader product context

The analytical experience was therefore treated as part of a larger decision-making workflow rather than as an isolated reporting feature.

---

## Comparison Experience

Comparison was identified as an important product capability because users may need to evaluate differences between relevant information sets.

The solution considered comparison workflows that could help users:

- Compare relevant entities or information
- Identify meaningful differences
- Recognize trends and patterns
- Support further investigation
- Improve decision-making

The comparison capability was designed to complement the analytics experience rather than operate as a separate workflow.

---

## Reporting Experience

Reporting was designed to convert analytical information into a structured and useful output.

The reporting workflow considered:

- Selecting relevant information
- Defining the reporting context
- Presenting information in a structured format
- Supporting sharing or further use of the output

The objective was to reduce the effort required to manually compile information for reporting purposes.

---

## AI-Assisted Experience

AI opportunities were evaluated as part of the product solution rather than as standalone technology features.

Potential AI-assisted capabilities included:

- Reducing repetitive analysis
- Identifying meaningful patterns
- Surfacing relevant insights
- Interpreting complex information
- Providing contextual assistance
- Supporting users in understanding analytical outputs

The product approach was to introduce AI only where it could improve an existing user workflow or reduce meaningful user effort.

AI capabilities were therefore treated as an enhancement to the product experience rather than the primary product proposition.

---

## User Workflow to Product Capability Mapping

The solution connected major user needs with corresponding product capabilities.

| User Need | Product Capability |
|---|---|
| Understand information quickly | Dashboard / Overview |
| Navigate different levels of detail | Structured Navigation |
| Explore information | Analytics |
| Identify differences | Comparison |
| Generate useful outputs | Reporting |
| Reduce manual analysis | AI Assistance |
| Make informed decisions | Insights and Analytical Views |

This mapping helped maintain a clear connection between the original user problem and the proposed product solution.

---

## MVP Considerations

The solution was considered incrementally so that the product could deliver value without requiring every potential capability to be available simultaneously.

The initial product focus was on capabilities that directly supported core user workflows:

1. Information overview
2. Structured navigation
3. Analytics
4. Comparison
5. Reporting

AI-assisted capabilities were considered progressively based on their ability to solve validated user problems and reduce manual effort.

This approach helped balance user value, product complexity, and delivery feasibility.

---

## Product Design Decisions

Several product decisions were driven by the discovery and requirements work:

### 1. Structure information before adding complexity

Complex information should be organized into understandable views before introducing additional analytical functionality.

### 2. Support progressive exploration

Users should be able to start with a high-level understanding and move into deeper analysis when required.

### 3. Connect features to workflows

Analytics, comparison, reporting, and AI capabilities should support connected user workflows rather than operate as isolated features.

### 4. Prioritize meaningful AI use cases

AI should be introduced where it can reduce effort, improve interpretation, or provide useful contextual assistance.

### 5. Design for incremental delivery

The product should support an MVP-first approach while leaving room for future analytical and AI capabilities.

---

## Constraints and Considerations

The solution was developed with several product considerations in mind:

- Complex information could create cognitive overload
- Different users may require different levels of detail
- Analytical capabilities need clear user objectives
- AI outputs require appropriate levels of trust and validation
- Product complexity should be controlled as capabilities expand
- Dependencies between information, analytics, reporting, and AI need to be considered during delivery

These considerations influenced the product structure and the sequencing of capabilities.

---

## Product Perspective

The solution design demonstrated the transition from identifying a problem to defining a practical product experience.

The key product principle was:

> **Design the product around the user's decision-making workflow, not around individual features.**

This ensured that information, analytics, comparison, reporting, and AI capabilities remained connected to the underlying user and business problems.
