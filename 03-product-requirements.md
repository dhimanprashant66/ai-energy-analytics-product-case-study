# Product Requirements

## Purpose

The objective of the requirements process was to translate identified user needs, business objectives, and product opportunities into clear and actionable product requirements.

The focus was on creating enough clarity for design and engineering teams while maintaining flexibility for iterative product development.

---

## Requirement Definition Approach

My approach to requirements included:

- Understanding the underlying user and business problem
- Defining the expected product outcome
- Identifying functional requirements
- Breaking broader requirements into manageable product increments
- Defining user stories and acceptance criteria
- Aligning requirements with user workflows
- Validating requirements with relevant stakeholders
- Refining requirements based on feedback and feasibility

The goal was to ensure that requirements described the problem and expected outcome clearly rather than prescribing unnecessary implementation details.

---

## Key Requirement Areas

The product requirements covered areas such as:

| Requirement Area | Product Focus |
|---|---|
| Information Experience | Present complex information in a clear and structured way |
| Navigation | Enable users to move efficiently across different levels of detail |
| Analytics | Support meaningful analysis and exploration |
| Comparison | Enable users to compare relevant information |
| Reporting | Provide structured and useful reporting workflows |
| User Experience | Simplify key workflows and reduce unnecessary complexity |
| AI Opportunities | Define potential AI-assisted experiences based on user needs |
| Product Analytics | Establish measures for adoption and engagement |

---

## User Stories

Requirements were translated into user stories to provide a user-centric view of expected functionality.

Typical structure:

> As a [user], I want to [perform an action], so that I can [achieve an outcome].

Examples of sanitized product requirements included:

- As a user, I want to access relevant information quickly so that I can understand the current situation without navigating through unnecessary complexity.
- As a user, I want to explore information at different levels of detail so that I can move from a high-level view to deeper analysis when required.
- As a user, I want to compare relevant information so that I can identify meaningful differences or trends.
- As a user, I want to generate structured reports so that I can share or review information in a consistent format.
- As a user, I want relevant insights to be surfaced from complex information so that I can reduce manual analysis effort.

---

## Acceptance Criteria

Acceptance criteria were used to establish a shared understanding of when a requirement could be considered complete.

Criteria focused on:

- Expected user behavior
- Required information or output
- Workflow conditions
- Validation rules
- Error and edge-case handling
- Usability expectations
- Business rules where applicable

This helped reduce ambiguity between product, design, engineering, and testing teams.

---

## Prioritization

Requirements were evaluated based on:

- User value
- Business impact
- Frequency of the underlying problem
- Product relevance
- Delivery feasibility
- Dependencies
- Expected effort
- Alignment with product priorities

This helped separate high-value product capabilities from lower-priority enhancements and supported incremental delivery.

---

## Requirement Validation

Before moving requirements into delivery, I worked with relevant stakeholders and cross-functional teams to validate:

- Problem and user need
- Expected product behavior
- Scope and priority
- Design implications
- Technical feasibility
- Dependencies
- Acceptance criteria

Feedback from these discussions was incorporated into the requirements before development.

---

## From Requirements to Delivery

The finalized requirements were translated into actionable backlog items and supported through the delivery lifecycle.

This included:

1. Requirement definition
2. User story creation
3. Acceptance criteria
4. Design collaboration
5. Backlog refinement
6. Sprint planning
7. Development support
8. Testing and validation
9. UAT support
10. Requirement refinement based on feedback

The focus was on maintaining a clear connection between the original user problem, the product requirement, and the delivered outcome.

---

## Product Perspective

The requirements process reinforced an important product principle:

**Good requirements should create clarity without unnecessarily limiting the solution.**

My focus was therefore on clearly defining the user problem, expected outcome, and success criteria while collaborating with design and engineering teams on the best way to achieve that outcome.
